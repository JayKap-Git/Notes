How the course builds

  1. Why the Gita: it is the essence of the Vedas, heard from a flawless source.
  2. How to learn it: from a bona fide teacher in disciplic succession, with humility, inquiry, service and faith.
  3. Background: the Mahabharata story up to the war at Kurukshetra.
  4. Chapter 1: Duryodhana’s fear and Dhritarashtra’s attachment set against Arjuna’s sensitivity.
  5. Arjuna’s dilemma: five good qualities, misdirected, freeze him — which is where Krishna’s teaching begins.

The thread through everything: move from Duryodhana-consciousness to Arjuna-consciousness, and act from duty (dharma) rather than impulse.

Summary

The Gita is the condensed essence of the Vedas. The Vedas were written down about 5000 years ago only because human memory and consciousness had declined. The Gita covers five topics: God (Ishvara), the soul (Jiva), material nature (Prakriti), time (Kala) and action (Karma).

Summary

Three sources of knowledge: perception, inference and testimony. Perception carries the “four defects” (imperfect senses, illusion, mistakes, cheating). Inference is built on perception, so it inherits those defects.

Qualification of disciple / student — BG 4.34

tad viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā / upadekṣyanti te…
  1. Humble surrender → open mind / eager to hear (“I do not know, let me try to understand what my teacher is speaking”).
  2. Inquisitiveness → have the spirit of inquiry; interested to understand the subject matter; ask doubts wherever they come.
  3. Submissive service → the most important service a student can render is to hear the teachings from the teacher.

The student, per BG 4.34, needs humility, questioning, service and truthfulness — and, per 4.39, faith.

Each of Arjuna’s reasons is turned around. Real compassion means saving the people from Duryodhana’s rule. The Kauravas meet every mark of an ātatāyī (aggressor), so killing them incurs no sin. Bhishma and Drona would be the ones most disappointed if he fled. A bad king corrupts his subjects. And indecision has an obvious cure: Krishna is right in front of him.

This sets up Chapter 2, where Arjuna finally asks Krishna for guidance.

The capital-punishment argument is the lecturer’s interpretation; it’s a contested topic in ethics and law.
